ux research for ai products

hi, i'm bridget.

i'm a ux researcher studying human-computer interaction at njit, and i want to research ai products: how people use them, when they trust them, and whether their answers hold up against the evidence.

my background is in psychology, which is why my work starts with people. i've run diary studies at njit, worked on navigation and vendor usability at asapp healthcare, and designed and built an ai-assisted research dashboard during my ux research internship at fiserv. i graduate in december 2027.

throughline

an ai app that answers a therapist's questions about a client using only that client's session transcripts, linking every claim to the passage it came from.

when the transcripts don't cover something, the app says so instead of guessing, because an invented detail in a clinical record could mislead the next person who reads it. i designed it for is 373 and host it on my own server, and i'm now checking whether each cited passage actually supports the claim it's attached to. it runs on six fictional transcripts, so no real client data is involved.

evaluation result: coming once the checks run (december 2026).
